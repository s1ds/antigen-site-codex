Phase 01

The Input

Project Brief · GET–TO–BY

L'Oréal's core challenge is to grow dermo-cosmetics in Israel from a still-underdeveloped niche into a mainstream facial skincare choice. The category has strong momentum, but Israel is behind Europe: dermo-cosmetics are about 8% of the Israeli beauty market versus roughly 13% in Europe. The business problem is not just competing with other dermo brands — it is converting consumers who currently buy from mass and prestige skincare, including L'Oréal Paris, Kiehl's, and broader beauty brands that are eroding the division's share. The sharper conversion challenge: why should consumers outside dermo-cosmetics choose Vichy, La Roche-Posay, or CeraVe specifically? The brands have credibility, loyalty, and engagement among users, but spontaneous awareness is still low — and Vichy has an additional issue: it is well known and appreciated, but that awareness is not converting strongly enough into trial.

GET

Mass and prestige skincare consumers in Israel who perceive dermo-cosmetics as a niche category.

TO

Adopt L'Oréal dermo-brands as their primary, mainstream facial skincare choice over beauty competitors.

BY

Executing category-growth strategies that bridge awareness gaps and recruit users from mass-market brands.

Phase 02

Insight

The Sick-Skin Tax

They want the science.
They reject the scientist.

In Israel's hyper-social, high-expressive, status-driven culture, skincare is not a private health routine — it is an act of public vanity.

The Aspirational Drive
"I want clinical-grade efficacy to look and feel flawless."

Consumers crave active ingredients — Hyaluronic Acid, Retinol, Vitamin C.

VS
The Pathological Barrier
"If I buy a dermo brand, it means my skin is broken or diseased."

Buying at the pharmacy counter feels like a public declaration of a "problem."

Why This Kills Our Safe Ideas
  • Stop trying to make "pharmacy" look cool. The pharmacy is where people buy medicine. We cannot change the retail DNA — trying is a losing battle.
  • Stop marketing "skin health." Start marketing "active superiority." "Healthy skin" is a boring, defensive benefit that sounds like recovery. We must pivot from remedial solution to aspirational weapon.
  • The Vichy Paradox: High awareness, low trial — people respect its medical credentials but don't think they're "sick" enough to need it. We must transition Vichy from a "skin doctor" to a "skin architect."
Phase 03

Provocation

Burn the White Coat

If your skin isn't broken,
why would you buy a medicine?

The Israeli dermo-cosmetics market is stagnant at 8% because we are trapped in a self-inflicted medical ghetto. By relying on sterile white coats, clinical jargon, and problem-solution marketing, we've convinced consumers that La Roche-Posay, CeraVe, and Vichy are only for "sick" skin. To unlock the remaining 5% of the market, we must kill the medical aesthetic and start selling high-performance cosmetic dominance.

Phase 06

Execution

The Skincare Freier Audit · הנתיחה של הביוטי

Exposing Prestige Illusion to Make Clinical the Ultimate Flex

The Israeli consumer prides themselves on one core cultural trait: not being a Freier (a sucker). Yet millions spend hundreds of shekels on prestige, gold-flaked, heavily perfumed creams that do nothing for cellular skin health. To move dermo-cosmetics from 8% to 13%+, we will not act like a boring medical alternative — we win by exposing the vanity tax of mass and prestige beauty.

The Safe Idea to Kill

Partnering with friendly beauty influencers for "Get Ready With Me" videos showing La Roche-Posay in a daily glam routine, paired with sterile PR-release clinical trials. It blends into background noise — Israeli consumers have high ad-skepticism, and influencer flat-lays don't change systemic purchasing habits.

A
The Public Outcry
The Skincare Autopsy

A controversial, unbranded PR stunt: independent cosmetic chemists and uncompromising Israeli digital creators "dissect" the ingredient lists of Israel's top five best-selling luxury and mass-market facial creams via aggressive short-form teardowns. The hook: "You paid 450 NIS for perfume and a glass jar. Here is the 120 NIS clinical equivalent that actually penetrates your dermis."

B
The Active Ingredient Guarantee
The Skincare Sucker Checker

An interactive digital tool and WhatsApp hotline. Consumers upload a photo of their current moisturizer's ingredient list; our AI-driven engine instantly analyzes the formulation, highlights filler ingredients in red, and matches their skin goal to the scientifically superior CeraVe, LRP, or Vichy formulation — at a fraction of the price.

C
Selective Pharmacy Activation
The Prestige Exchange

For one week, consumers bring empty luxury or mass-market jars to dermo-cosmetic counters at Super-Pharm and Be. In return: a "Clinical Upgrade" — a full-sized L'Oréal dermo product matched to their needs, plus a diagnostic receipt showing scientific superiority. The pharmacy counter becomes an active intervention zone, not a passive shelf.

01
Slide 01 · Challenge
Closing the 5% Gap
  • The Market Disconnect: Dermo-cosmetics represent 13% of the European beauty market but only 8% in Israel — millions in unrealized value.

02

The Clinical vs. Cosmetic Paradox

  • The Friction: Mass-market skincare sells "hope in a jar" with high-sensory marketing; dermo communicates through clinical jargon that feels cold and transactional.
  • The Core Truth: Consumers want pharmaceutical-grade results wrapped in a luxury cosmetic experience — not "pharmaceutical products."
03

  • The Shift: Stop positioning dermo as the solution for "problematic skin." Start positioning it as the performance upgrade for all skin.

  • The Action: Shift messaging from defensive correction ("fix your skin") to offensive optimization ("upgrade your active ingredients").
