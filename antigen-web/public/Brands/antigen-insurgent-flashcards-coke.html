Enquiry

What you told me

"Coca-Cola is failing to convert Gen Z into regular consumers despite claiming 'high brand love.' This term 'brand love' is corporate fluff; I need the mechanical reality of how sentiment is measured if it results in zero transactional velocity. You are effectively saying people like the idea of the product but refuse to put it in their mouths."

What I heard

You are assuming that brand sentiment is a leading indicator of sales, that Gen Z's definition of 'habit' aligns with your current distribution model, and that the product's value proposition is still relevant if only the 'recruitment' strategy is fixed. You assume the problem lies in the bridge between affinity and action, rather than the product itself.

The Gap

There is a fundamental contradiction between 'high brand love' and a lack of 'trial.' If they love it, they would try it. This suggests 'brand love' is a vanity metric masking a deeper irrelevance. You have also omitted the mechanical barriers: price elasticity, health-conscious substitution, and the fact that a 100-year-old sugar-water habit may be culturally incompatible with Gen Z's daily mechanics.
