# ANTIGEN V3 Style Guide

## Source Reading

This style guide is based on the attached ANTIGEN credentials presentation. The deck establishes a provocative brand system around a post-algorithm organism built to tackle stagnation and sameness through a purpose-built configuration of minds and machines. Its visual character is defined by red/magenta manifesto fields, oversized uppercase typography, white evidence layouts, chart/screenshot fragments, section navigation, and insurgent image treatment.

## Brand Personality

ANTIGEN should feel like a strategic insurgency, not a consultancy brand deck. It is sharp, adversarial, intelligent, editorial, and deliberately uncomfortable. The brand exists as a reaction against algorithmic homogeneity, legacy operating models, short-termism, and the false promise that automation alone can solve growth.

The emotional register is not friendly innovation. It is controlled provocation. The audience should feel that a system is being challenged, not that a service is being politely introduced.

## Visual Mood

The mood is manifesto plus evidence room. One side of the system is visceral, red, human, masked, distorted, theatrical, rebellious. The other side is white, diagnostic, chart-led, analytical, and precise. The best ANTIGEN assets should move between these two modes: impact, then proof; accusation, then architecture; rupture, then method.

## Logo Treatment

Use the ANTIGEN wordmark in black or white depending on contrast. It may be partially obscured by a symbol, overlaid with imagery, or placed inside a red field. Do not trap it in a polished corporate lockup unless necessary. The wordmark can behave as part of the composition rather than as a sacred isolated asset.

Minimum clear space should equal the height of the lowercase “a” around the logo when used conventionally. In manifesto compositions, controlled violation is allowed: cropping, collision, and overlap are acceptable when the intent is clear.

## Color Usage

Red and magenta are the signature field. They should dominate hero, divider, manifesto, and high-emotion assets. White and off-white are the analytical field. Black gives weight and severity. Yellow is a solution signal and should be used sparingly. Blue is a rare contrast accent for emphasis or section distinction.

Never overuse the full palette at once. The system works best when one dominant territory controls the asset: red manifesto, white evidence, black intervention, or yellow signal.

## Typography Usage

Typography is the principal visual device. Headlines should be large enough to feel physically present. Use uppercase display type for claims and compressed explanatory type for proof. The primary typeface is Montserrat, with Montserrat SemiBold reflected in the source deck theme. For web and more expressive assets, a condensed display typeface such as Bebas Neue or Oswald can approximate the poster-like force seen in the deck.

Headlines should use tight leading and negative tracking. Do not center everything. Let text align to the left, bleed off an edge, or collide with image zones. Small navigation text should sit at the top and feel like a system marker.

## Composition

The system prefers strong asymmetry. Large blocks of type can occupy the left two-thirds of a canvas. Evidence, diagrams, or images often sit in the right third or right half. Full-bleed red covers can place a human figure in the center while text overwhelms or intersects the image.

For analytical pages, use wide white space and one dominant claim. Avoid busy consulting-slide density. When density is necessary, make it intentional, as in the word wall pattern where complexity is used as a background to be overpowered by a simpler critique.

## Image Direction

Images should not be decorative stock. Select images that carry unease, transformation, ritual, rebellion, pressure, or system failure. Human subjects may be masked, cropped, red-washed, silhouetted, or partially hidden by type. Charts and screenshots should be treated as evidence objects, not dashboard decoration.

Preferred treatments include red overlays, black multiply shadows, grayscale conversion, high contrast, central cropping, partial obstruction, and oversized text layered across the image.

## Data and Evidence

The deck uses data to support the argument around sameness, stagnation, suppression, and legacy models. Data should be framed as proof of a strategic problem. Use large red titles, short explanatory captions, and isolated charts. Do not build generic dashboard panels. Use evidence blocks, pinned screenshot cards, and annotated fragments.

## Section System

Use the section structure from the deck as a recurring navigation device:

`0. PRELUDE`  
`1. PURPOSE`  
`2. PHILOSOPHY`  
`3. PRODUCT`  
`4. PEOPLE`

The current section should be highlighted with yellow on red backgrounds or red on white backgrounds. The nav should remain small and top-aligned.

## Component Rules

Hero manifesto sections should contain one large claim and one short explanatory line. Evidence sections should contain one claim, one proof object, and one conclusion. Force cards should use the triptych of sameness, stagnation, and suppression. Solution sections may use yellow torn-signal shapes to represent agents of growth.

Buttons should be sharp, rectangular, uppercase, and bordered. Avoid pill buttons. Cards should be rectangular with minimal shadows or brutal hard shadows. Corners should generally remain square.

## Copy Style

Copy should be compressed and declarative. Use enemy/problem language openly. Avoid generic transformation jargon unless deliberately critiquing it. The strongest lines use contrast: “speed without direction,” “infinite capability, finite courage,” “post-algorithm problems with a pre-internet factory.”

Preferred copy structure: name the force, define the trap, show the evidence, introduce the insurgent alternative.

## Practical Asset Templates

### Homepage Hero

Red/magenta background. Full-bleed image or shadow figure. Oversized headline: `WE BATTLE STAGNATION WITH INSURGENCE`. Subline: `A post-algorithm organism built to solve hard growth problems with purpose-built configurations of minds and machines.` CTA: `Bring us a hard problem`.

### Evidence Page

White background. Left column: force title and diagnosis. Right column: chart, screenshot, statistic, or annotated visual. Small top section nav. Red title, black body copy, grey caption.

### Three Forces Spread

Three equal columns: sameness, stagnation, suppression. Each contains a category label, large red title, one evidence object, and one short diagnosis.

### Legacy Model Page

White background. Large red headline on left. Diagram or trap illustration on right. Small paragraph beneath headline explaining how the old model incentivizes complexity and inefficiency.

### Agents of Growth Page

White or black background. Large black or white claim on one side. Yellow torn shape on the other containing growth agents: LLMs reducing entry barriers, insurgent minds freed by layoffs, latency between problems and solvers, democratized bleeding-edge technology.

## Quality Control

An ANTIGEN asset is successful when it feels impossible to mistake for a legacy agency deck. It should have a point of view before it has decoration. It should look like it is attacking sameness while proving its case with enough discipline to be credible.
